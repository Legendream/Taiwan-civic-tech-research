# Reader Feedback

## 1. Hard to understand

- **Summary - `### 1. Projects stall because of momentum, not a lack of people`**: "And the more say someone has in a project, the more likely they are to burn out: **6 of the 10** who started their own project said core-member burnout clearly held them back, compared with **1 of 8** who joined a project someone else led."
  Comparing very small, unequal subgroups (6 of 10 versus 1 of 8) without accounting for the remaining Builders makes it hard to evaluate the strength of the pattern.

- **Summary - `### 2. A shared goal brings people in, and keeps them there`**: "Some motives grew over time: building skills and a portfolio went from 6 people to 9."
  Describing a difference of three individuals as growth "over time" is confusing without knowing whether this comes from a multi-year study or a single retrospective questionnaire.

- **Summary - `### 3. Newcomers get stuck at "where do I start?", not "I can't code"`**: "Issue research and project management were the most common skills, at 51% each."
  It is unclear whether "issue research" refers to studying public policy topics or handling technical bug tickets on a development board.

- **Summary - `### 4. Funders want a clearly explained problem, and early ideas struggle most`**: "27 respondents had funded civic tech projects, and 26 of them were already part of the community."
  A first-time reader cannot tell whether "funded" refers to institutional grants, individual donations, or volunteers paying out-of-pocket project costs.

- **Full Report - `[summary-03]`**: "Progress that stops and starts, results that don't land, and a few people holding on for a long time: together, these three are the biggest obstacle."
  Colloquial phrases like "results that don't land" and "holding on for a long time" leave it unclear whether the obstacle is lack of adoption, government inaction, or volunteer fatigue.

- **Full Report - `[ch2-1-04]`**: "**A large group sticks to one thing, while a small group does almost everything**, and the spread in between is uneven."
  The casual expressions "sticks to one thing" and "does almost everything" obscure how participants actually distribute across the five project roles.

- **Full Report - `[ch2-1-05]`**: "The five options run from the least to the most say over a project's direction: Follow, Report, Join, Co-design and Start (another 6 people chose "None of these fits me", which is left off this scale)."
  Placing "Report" between "Follow" and "Join" along a spectrum of project influence is baffling without a definition of what "Report" entails.

- **Full Report - `[ch3-1-04]`**: "These three may feed into each other: when momentum breaks, results are slow to appear; without results, it is harder to show the project is worth the effort; fewer people step in, and the load falls back on the few."
  It suggests a speculative causal cycle between three survey options without indicating whether respondents reported this chain or if it is an author hypothesis.

- **Full Report - `[ch3-3-03]`**: "Now look at the item "Newcomers or people who can't code don't know how they can help". The people who feel it most are at the shallow end: 2 of 6 (33%) in the Report group, 1 of 5 (20%) in the Follow group, 1 of 8 (12%) who joined someone else's project, 0% (0/18) in the Co-design group, and 1 of 10 (10%) who started a project."
  Mixing two completely different classification frameworks (the ladder of involvement and project positions) in one comparative list makes the breakdown very difficult to parse.

- **Full Report - `[ch4-1-04]`**: ""Retention rate" means the same person chose the same motive both times. "Net change" looks at how the number of people rose or fell: those who chose a motive when they first joined, plus those who picked it up later, minus those who no longer give it as a reason."
  The formula described for "net change" calculates the final head count rather than a rate of change, making the mathematical explanation confusing.

- **Full Report - `[ch5-3-03]`**: "Asked how much time they spent on the project in the past 30 days: 1 to 5 hours was the most common answer (32%), followed by 6 to 20 hours and almost no time, tied at 23% each; 21 hours or more came to 21%."
  Treating "almost no time" as a tied category alongside numerical hour ranges is confusing without a clear threshold defining "almost no time".

- **Full Report - `[rec-1-1-08]`**: "The results show that all five candidate article topics fall into "Attractive", with "How to get what you built into real use, including within government" closest to "Performance"."
  Applying product-management terms like "Attractive" and "Performance" to educational topics without earlier context makes the takeaway hard to grasp.

- **Full Report - `[reflect-1-04]`**: "The 27 funders represent the views of funders (Chapter 6)"
  Framing this statement as the report's conclusion in the table contradicts the earlier findings showing these funders are internal community members rather than external funders.

## 2. Missing context

- **Background on g0v.tw (`1_summary.md` Paragraph 2; `2_full_report.md` `[intro-02]`, `[about-05]`)**: A Malaysian reader does not know what g0v.tw represents in Taiwan, its history, or why the name uses a zero instead of the letter "o" in `.gov.tw`.
- **Identity and nature of "Jothon" (`1_summary.md` Paragraph 2; `2_full_report.md` `[top-01]`, `[intro-02]`, `[intro-03]`)**: The name appears without explanation of what kind of entity it is, what the romanized word means, or its relationship to the wider g0v.tw community.
- **References to "vTaiwan" and the "mask map" (`2_full_report.md` `[intro-04]`)**: These projects are cited as standard examples of civic tech, but a reader from outside Taiwan has no background on what they are, how they operated, or how they interfaced with government policy and data.
- **Meaning of "public-private collaboration" (`1_summary.md` Section 6; `2_full_report.md` `[ch2-2-03]`, `[ch2-2-04]`, `[ch7-3-02]`, `[ch7-3-04]`)**: In international and Commonwealth contexts, public-private collaboration or partnerships typically refer to commercial state contracts, so background is needed to understand how Taiwanese civil society and civil servants collaborate directly on civic tools.
- **Involvement of civil servants (`2_full_report.md` `[ch2-2-03]`)**: A reader from a system where civil servants cannot participate in civil society needs background to understand how Taiwanese public sector employees interact with open civic tech hackathons.
- **Geography and regional dynamics (`2_full_report.md` `[ch1-02]`, `[ch1-1-09]`, `[reflect-1-04]`)**: Mentions of Taipei, New Taipei, Taichung, Tainan, Kaohsiung, and Hualien lack geographic context explaining the concentration of resources in the northern capital area versus central, southern, and eastern regions.
- **Platform purpose of "Civic Tech Taiwan" (`1_summary.md` Section 6, Explore more; `2_full_report.md` `[intro-05]`, `[rec-1-3-05]`)**: It is described as a database, but readers need background on whether it operates as an active repository, a project directory, or an informational portal.
- **The cultural metaphor of "jumping in" (`2_full_report.md` `[summary-03]`)**: The term "jump in" is placed in quotation marks without explaining the Taiwanese open-source community metaphor of proposing and taking ownership of tasks (坑 / 跳坑).
- **Structure of civic tech funding in Taiwan (`1_summary.md` Section 4; `2_full_report.md` `[ch6-1-02]`, `[ch6-1-06]`)**: A reader needs background on how civic tech is financed in Taiwan to understand why almost all surveyed "funders" are community insiders rather than independent foundations or public grants.

## 3. Unclear terms

- **"Jothon"**: The label is never defined or translated, and it is capitalized inconsistently across the text as "Jothon" (e.g., `[top-01]`, `[intro-02]`) and lowercase "jothon" (e.g., `[about-02]`, `[about-06]`).
- **Participant categorization frameworks**: The report introduces "The Curious", "The Users", and "The Builders" (`1_summary.md` lines 18–21; `2_full_report.md` `[ch1-1-03]`), then later switches to a five-tier ladder ("Follow, Report, Join, Co-design, Start" in `[ch2-1-05]`), and later a three-tier leadership scale ("Joined", "Define with others", "Started it myself" in `[ch3-2-03]`), without explaining how these categories correlate or overlap.
- **"Report"**: Used as a distinct level of participation in `[ch2-1-05]` and `[ch3-3-03]` without explanation of whether it refers to reporting software bugs, submitting administrative updates, or contributing journalistic reports.
- **"Issue research"**: Used in `1_summary.md` (line 52) and `2_full_report.md` (`[ch5-2-02]`, `[ch5-2-03]`, `[ch5-2-04]`) without specifying whether it refers to social and public policy research or software issue tracking.
- **"Funders" vs. "mobilizing resources" vs. "putting money into"**: The report uses "funded" (`1_summary.md` line 59; `2_full_report.md` `[ch6-1-04]`), "put money into" (`[summary-09]`), and "provided money or mobilized resources" (`[ch2-1-03]`) interchangeably, conflating financial sponsorship with non-monetary resource mobilization.
- **"Core members"**: Introduced as a key group facing burnout (`1_summary.md` lines 11, 33; `2_full_report.md` `[summary-02]`, `[summary-03]`, `[ch3-1-02]`, `[ch3-2-02]`), but never explicitly defined or distinguished from the broader group of 47 Builders.
- **"Say" / "How much say you have"**: Used repeatedly as an informal variable for decision-making power (`[ch3-2-03]`, `[ch3-2-04]`, `[ch3-3-04]`, `[rec-1-4-06]`) without being operationalized as a formal research measure.
- **"Kano analysis (the Kano model)"**: Introduced abruptly in `[rec-1-1-03]` with specialized terms ("Attractive", "Performance", "Must-be", "Indifferent") without explaining the methodology or why a product feature model is used to assess informational articles.
- **Untranslated Chinese text**:
  - `表格標題：` appears as raw, untranslated table label prefixes in `[ch3-2-03]`, `[ch3-4-03]`, `[ch4-1-03]`, and `[ch6-1-03]`.
  - `臺灣公民科技推廣問卷填寫者` appears untranslated in `[intro-02]` and `[about-03]`.
  - `零時政府` appears untranslated in `[about-05]`.
  - `鄭婷宇` appears untranslated in `[about-06]`.

## 4. Anything that sounds unnatural in English

- **`1_summary.md` - `### 1. Projects stall because of momentum, not a lack of people`**: "Not finding partners came only fourth (23%)."
  "Came only fourth" is unidiomatic; natural English would use "ranked only fourth" or "came in fourth".

- **`1_summary.md` - `### 1. Projects stall because of momentum, not a lack of people`**: "And the more say someone has in a project, the more likely they are to burn out: **6 of the 10** who started their own project said core-member burnout clearly held them back, compared with **1 of 8** who joined a project someone else led."
  Starting an analytical sentence with "And the more say someone has..." sounds overly conversational, and "say" functions awkwardly as a formal noun for decision-making authority.

- **`1_summary.md` - `### 3. Newcomers get stuck at "where do I start?", not "I can't code"`**: "Feel they would need to code: **35%**, fourth place"
  "Feel they would need to code" is a fragmented clause awkwardly punctuated with trailing statistics.

- **`1_summary.md` - `### 4. Funders want a clearly explained problem, and early ideas struggle most`**: "Only 3 said the same about projects that already have a prototype."
  "Said the same about" is too colloquial and vague for a formal report; it should state the specific opinion directly.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[summary-03]`**: "This is quite likely because more say usually means more effort and time, which makes people more tired."
  "Which makes people more tired" sounds childish and overly informal for an analytical report on community burnout.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[summary-07]`**: "65% of all respondents regularly see news from at least one official [g0v.tw](https://g0v.tw/) channel, but **seeing the news does not mean knowing what you can do, or knowing someone you can ask**."
  Beginning a sentence with a bare numeral ("65%") violates standard English style conventions, and "seeing the news" is an awkward way to describe following community announcements.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch1-2-02]`**: "47% of respondents have never been to a [g0v.tw](https://g0v.tw/) event: getting the news does not mean knowing what you can do, or having someone to ask."
  Starting with a bare numeral ("47%") is nonstandard in formal writing, and linking an attendance statistic to a general takeaway via a colon feels disjointed.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch2-1-04]`**: "**A large group sticks to one thing, while a small group does almost everything**, and the spread in between is uneven."
  "Sticks to one thing" and "does almost everything" read like casual speech rather than professional research prose.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch3-1-04]`**: ""Can't find partners who want to work on it together", the one most people would think of first, came fourth."
  "The one most people would think of first" is an informal conversational interjection, and "came fourth" should be "ranked fourth".

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch3-2-04]`**: "Among these respondents, those more deeply involved and with more say were more likely to face core-member burnout (quite likely their own), and more worried that the results would not make a difference."
  The parenthetical "(quite likely their own)" reads like casual speculation, and the sentence loses parallel structure between "were more likely to face" and "and more worried".

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch3-3-03]`**: "Now look at the item "Newcomers or people who can't code don't know how they can help"."
  "Now look at the item..." sounds like an oral presentation slide script rather than a written research document.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch3-3-04]`**: "As people's say in a project grows, this worry broadly tends to be lower."
  Describing "say" as something that "grows" is unidiomatic, and "broadly tends to be lower" stacks redundant qualifying adverbs.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch3-4-05]`**: "Adding the 6 who took no particular action or only described the difficulty, 11 of the 28 either chose to lower their expectations or did nothing in particular."
  "Adding the 6..." is a dangling participle that awkwardly attaches to "11 of the 28" rather than the researcher.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[ch4-1-03]`**: "表格標題：First motive → ongoing motive (the 47 Builders, more than one answer allowed)"
  Untranslated Chinese characters ("表格標題：") are left embedded in an English heading.

- **`2_full_report.md` - `[rec-1-1-09]`**: "This may reflect that people who have not engaged deeply with civic tech feel the pain of running a team and keeping it going less."
  Placing "less" at the very end of a lengthy verbal phrase creates an awkward sentence rhythm and makes comprehension difficult.
